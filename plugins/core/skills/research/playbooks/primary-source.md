# Primary-Source Research

How to analyze something when the user shares a derivative source — a video, blog post, thread, or article about someone else's idea or work.

## The Discipline

**The derivative is a claim about the original, not the original itself.** A blog post summarizing a research paper is someone's interpretation. A video walkthrough of a published system is a presentation, not the system. Analyze the derivative to understand the claims being made, then fetch and analyze the original to see what's actually there.

## The Sequence

1. **Identify the original.** Who created the actual work? Where did they publish it? (Gist, paper, repo, talk transcript, official documentation.)
2. **Fetch the original.** Read it end-to-end before analyzing.
3. **Diff against the derivative.** What did the derivative include, omit, flatten, or reinterpret? Structural elements present in the original but absent from the derivative are the most valuable findings — they're what the user would miss by reading only the derivative.
4. **Analyze from the primary source.** Build your assessment on what the original says, supplemented by the derivative's framing where it adds context.

## Why This Matters

Derivatives flatten. A video drops structural elements that don't present well visually. A blog emphasizes what the author found interesting, not necessarily what's architecturally important. The user shared the derivative because they encountered it — but they're asking you to help them understand the actual work.
