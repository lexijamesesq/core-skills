# Exploratory Research

How to learn a domain you don't know yet. The goal is understanding — building vocabulary, mapping the landscape, and forming a mental model the user can reason with.

## The Discipline

**You don't know the vocabulary yet.** Precise queries constructed from parametric knowledge reflect your vocabulary, not the domain's. Start broad and let the domain teach you its terms.

**The session is the unit of work, not the query.** Each result informs the next query. Evolving queries is correct behavior. A linear sequence of targeted lookups is the wrong shape for exploratory work.

**Follow information scent.** Results rich with new vocabulary and unfamiliar structure warrant investigation before narrowing. Sparse results mean go broader or try adjacent vocabulary. The decision at each step is narrow (stay in this patch) vs pivot (new patch).

## The Funnel Applied to Exploration

### Stage 1: Landscape
Category-level queries. What exists in this space? What are the major categories? Who are the authorities? Do not use domain-specific terms you haven't acquired from results.

### Stage 2: Vocabulary
Extract terms and facets from Stage 1. Map the user's language to domain language. Identify the axes that differentiate options in this space.

### Stage 3: Narrowing
Use the terms the funnel produced. Combine 2-3 facets per query. Run parallel searches on adjacent vocabulary. Each result may introduce new vocabulary worth investigating.

### Stage 4: Synthesis
Organize what you've learned into a structure the user can navigate. Map the landscape, name the axes of differentiation, and surface the decisions that matter. Present the mental model, not a list of facts.

## Source Quality

Dig past first-page aggregators. SEO-optimized content with no byline or methodology is lead generation, not research. Chase specialist reviewers, community forums with real-use data, industry or academic sources, and the primary works behind derivative summaries. A source's authority comes from methodology and expertise, not search ranking.

## When to Stop Exploring

The transition from exploration to decision happens when:
- The vocabulary is stable (new queries aren't producing new terms)
- The landscape is mapped (you can name the major categories and their trade-offs)
- The user's constraints narrow the field to a tractable set

If the user pushes toward a decision before exploration is complete, name what's still unknown rather than manufacturing certainty.
