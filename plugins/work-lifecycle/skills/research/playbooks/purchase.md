# Purchase Research

How to find the right product to buy. Load domain context from the vault first — context pages and domain knowledge carry prior research, hard constraints, and validated preferences that prevent rediscovering established ground.

## Before Searching

**Extract hard constraints from the user's own words.** A stated tool, format, or ingredient eliminates options before you search. A stated application method dictates the product form. A stated sensitivity narrows to specific exclusions, not broad categories. Honor constraints as stated — do not broaden or soften them.

**Name the ONE real problem and anchor on it.** Don't spread weight across solved or irrelevant axes. If the user's only real problem is one specific attribute, don't burn cycles on axes that are already decided or don't matter. Ask: "which axis is actually unresolved?"

**Ground every constraint at its stated weight.** A medium-weight nice-to-have is not a top-tier requirement. A single data point is not a category. Don't promote inference to a hard constraint.

## While Searching

**Use the funnel for exploratory purchases.** When the user is discovering what to buy, landscape scan first — what exists in this category, what are the axes of differentiation. Do not jump to specific products from parametric knowledge. When the user already knows the exact product (lookup mode), skip the funnel and go directly to validation — verify current availability, pricing, and formulation against live sources.

**Discover through domain-appropriate channels.** Keyword search against product databases produces generic, interchangeable results for many product categories. Discovery mechanisms vary by domain:
- Products evaluated visually or by feel: editorial roundups, styled photography, curated selections filtered by specific body/context/use-case
- Products evaluated by formulation or composition: ingredient databases, formulation-aware review sites, community testing data
- Products evaluated by performance: specialist reviewers with disclosed methodology, community forums with real-use data, professional tool reviews

If the vault carries a domain methodology, it specifies which discovery channels work for that domain. Use it.

**Evidence over marketing copy.** Verify claims against independent sources. Swatch or sample databases for visual attributes. Ingredient or spec databases for formulation and composition. Reviews by people with similar constraints for fit, performance, and real-world behavior.

**Decompose verification into targeted lookups.** Separate queries for availability, formulation/specs, and reviews. One query will not settle everything.

**Apply real-world constraints as first-class filters.** Where they buy, what they already own, what ecosystem they're in. These are as important as product attributes.

## Before Presenting

**Validate every factual claim against a live source at the point of presentation.** Product names change. Formulas get reformulated. Prices drift. Links go dead. Fetch the current page before asserting — even if you fetched it earlier in this session.

**Never carry a prior fact onto a reformulated product.** If you screened an earlier version, that screening is invalid for the current one.

**Gut-check the proposal against your own method.** Before presenting, re-read the user's constraints and check each candidate against them. This catches dead recommendations and constraint violations before the user does.

**Present as leads, not verdicts.** The final acceptance test belongs to ground truth (physical evaluation, real-world use), not to your confidence. Frame findings as candidates that meet criteria, with specific things to verify.

## Retrieval Failures

Anti-bot retail sites return 403 or minimal content to automated fetch. This is not "can't validate" — it means the retrieval method is wrong. Fall back to browser automation. SPA pages lazy-load results; text extraction returns only the initial batch — scroll or paginate to get the full set. Price-comparison sites and affiliate aggregators may show stale data; verify against the retailer's own page.

## Stop Rule

Two misses in a row — candidates that violate stated constraints, or facts that don't survive validation — means stop and realign on the framing. The problem is the search setup, not the execution.
